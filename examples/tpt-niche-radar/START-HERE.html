Niche Radar 4.0

What to make next for TPT, and when to publish it

Every keyword you look up in TPT’s Site Search Analytics is saved with its monthly searches, peak month and weekly curve. The season planner shows which demand peaks next and what to build for it.

Update an existing install, your research stays

  1. Optional safety copy. Open your current Niche Radar workspace and click Back up workspace.
  2. Extract this zip into the same folder you loaded in Chrome before, the one with manifest.json, and let it replace the files. Keep the folder where it is. Chrome ties the stored research to that folder.
  3. Open chrome://extensions and click the reload arrow on the Niche Radar card. Version 4 also runs on teacherspayteachers.com pages, so Chrome may list that site under its access.
  4. Reload any TPT tab that was already open.

Nothing is deleted or converted. Version 4 reads the same browser storage as version 3. On its first save it also keeps an untouched copy of your v3 workspace, which you can download any time from Scoring and help.

First install

  1. Extract the zip to a folder you will keep.
  2. Open chrome://extensions, switch on Developer mode and click Load unpacked.
  3. Choose the folder that contains manifest.json, then pin Niche Radar in the toolbar.

Collect keywords on TPT

  1. In your Seller Dashboard, open Site Search Analytics and search a keyword.
  2. The Niche Radar panel in the corner saves the keyword on its own: average searches per month, total searches for the last 365 days, resources, TPT’s label, the peak month and its share of the year, and all 53 weeks of the curve.
  3. Click Collect all 100 to save every page of related keywords, each with its own peak season. The panel only reads the page and turns pages. It never runs a search, so the searches TPT allows each day stay untouched.

What is in the workspace

  • Season planner. Demand by peak month for the next twelve months, the build window one to three months out, and the best keywords to build for it with a publish-by date.
  • Keywords. Every keyword with its season strip, score, average searches a month, searches, resources, TPT label and change. Filter by peak month, quick views like Peaking soon, or switch to cards or the opportunity map.
  • Keyword detail. The weekly curve with the peak marked, the publish date, why it scored what it did, a notebook with status, tags and notes, and the keywords TPT buyers search with it.
  • Saved. A board from Inbox to Published. Drag a card to change its status.
  • Search families. Keywords TPT reports as searched together with each lookup, plus groups that share a word.
  • Sources. Every CSV and lookup, with its date and search window. Set a window like “Last 30 days” on old exports and their monthly average appears.

How the numbers are read

  • The weekly curve is read from TPT’s chart and checked against TPT’s own figures. For “new year” the 53 weeks add up to the 95,516 searches TPT reports.
  • Average searches a month comes from TPT when it shows it. For related keywords and CSVs it is the total divided by the months in the search window, and it stays empty when the window is unknown.
  • A peak month comes from TPT first, then from the weekly curve. When there is neither, a peak is inferred from words like Halloween or back to school and shown as an outline.
  • Scores rank what to research first. They do not predict sales.
